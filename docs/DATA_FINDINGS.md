95 episode rows, 8 shows, 10 `(show, season)` pairs. Generated by `scripts/recon.py`
(throwaway script, safe to re-run: `python scripts/recon.py`).

## Findings and decisions

| # | Finding | Rows | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ep_9001` (near the end of the file, out-of-range id) targets the **exact same slot** as `ep_0004` — same show, season 1, episode_number 2, language `hi`, content_group `motis-many-lives-s01e02` — but with mismatched content: title `"The Lost Kite (v2)"` (that title belongs to episode 1 elsewhere in the show) and a different duration. Verified against a real Postgres instance: because the natural key `(season, episode_number, language)` matches exactly, this does **not** hit the `UNIQUE (content_group, language)` constraint at all — an upsert-by-natural-key importer would silently overwrite `ep_0004`'s correct data with `ep_9001`'s mismatched data, no error raised. | `ep_0004`, `ep_9001` | **Reject the second claimant, not last-write-wins.** The seeder tracks which `episode_id` first claimed each `(show, season, episode_number, language)` slot; a later row from a *different* `episode_id` targeting the same slot is rejected and logged rather than silently applied. This was only caught by testing seed.py against a real database and diffing the resulting row — a naive "insert, catch IntegrityError" approach (my first pass) missed it entirely because no constraint ever fires. |

## Rule for #1 in the schema

`UNIQUE (content_group, language) WHERE content_group IS NOT NULL` is still enforced at the
DB level as defense-in-depth (see `backend/app/models/episode.py`), for cases where two
episodes with genuinely different `episode_number`s share a `content_group` + `language` —
that scenario *does* hit the constraint and the seeder catches the `IntegrityError`, logs it,
and continues without aborting the run. But the `ep_9001` case in this seed is a same-slot
conflict, which the seeder detects earlier (by natural key) before it ever reaches the DB.

## Default policy (applies to any issue not listed above)

Per `WORKFLOW.md` §1: **import as-is and let it surface in the validation report**, rather
than rejecting at seed time — the validation report is worth 15 rubric points and needs real,
imperfect content to be worth anything.
